Most of what I know about my own research I learned by explaining it to someone
else, so I treat teaching and outreach as part of the work rather than beside it.

## Public astronomy

I present at the **W. J. McCallion Planetarium** at McMaster, running shows for
school groups and the general public. Some of my favourite sessions are with the
youngest visitors: I host kindergarten groups at the planetarium and design
space-themed craft and learning activities pitched at five-year-olds, which is a
harder and more rewarding constraint than any colloquium talk.

## Mentoring

I co-supervise students at several stages — undergraduate, MITACS summer, and
master's — on projects in maser modelling and superradiance. Four so far, with two
ongoing. Most of them arrive having never solved a differential equation
numerically, and the useful part of my job is less about the physics than about
showing them that getting stuck is the normal state of research rather than
evidence they are doing it wrong.

I also teach as a TA at McMaster, across Scientific Computing, introductory
mechanics, quantum mechanics, and electricity and magnetism, and previously at the
University of Tehran. I have advised student groups preparing teaching
presentations for an interdisciplinary science course.

## Community and service

I organize events for **Promoting Inclusivity in Physics &amp; Astronomy (PIPA)** at
McMaster — coffee hours, movie nights, the small recurring things that make a
department feel like somewhere people want to be.

I have served as a judge at the Canadian Undergraduate Physics Conference, chaired
and judged the astronomy session at the Physics &amp; Astronomy Graduate Symposium,
and sat on the local organizing committee for the Toronto Ultra-Cold Atom Network
meeting.
